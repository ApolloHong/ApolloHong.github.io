A grid in 100 dimensions is out of the question, so the equation is solved on simulated paths instead. The scheme simulates \(X=\sqrt{2}\,W\) forward, then steps backward in time, and at each step regresses the next value onto the current state. Huré, Pham and Warin use a neural network for that regression. Here a small feature basis takes its place so it runs instantly. The exact value comes from the Hopf–Cole transform.

Among all diffusions that turn noise into data, the bridge picks the one closest to Brownian motion. The static problem is solved with log-domain Sinkhorn, the same iterative proportional fitting behind my research at the MathsFI lab, where I use Schrödinger bridges to generate SPY return paths under stochastic volatility and jumps. Pairs are then drawn from the coupling and joined with Brownian bridges. With small \(\varepsilon\) the paths approach straight optimal-transport lines. With large \(\varepsilon\) they become diffuse and the coupling loses its structure.

\[dX_t=\frac{v-P_t}{\lambda\,(1-t)}\,dt,\qquad dZ_t=\sigma_u\,dW_t\]\[dP_t=\lambda\,\big(dX_t+dZ_t\big),\qquad \lambda=\frac{\sigma_v}{\sigma_u}\]\[\Sigma_t=\operatorname{Var}(v\mid\mathcal{F}_t)=\sigma_v^2\,(1-t)\]

This is a game between an insider who knows the value \(v\), a crowd of noise traders, and a competitive market maker who sets the price \(P_t=\mathbb{E}[v\mid\text{order flow}]\). In equilibrium the insider trades gradually and hides inside the noise. The price becomes a Brownian bridge to \(v\). More noise trading gives a deeper market (smaller \(\lambda\)) and a larger insider profit, yet information still arrives at exactly the same linear rate.

Kyle, Continuous auctions and insider trading, Econometrica 1985 · Back, Insider trading in continuous time, RFS 1992.
