01 / Approach

How I look at a problem

Financial data has a low signal-to-noise ratio, is non-stationary, and pushes back once you trade on it. These are the rules I work by.

p(θ | data) ∝ p(data | θ) · p(θ)

Every estimate comes with its uncertainty

A point forecast on its own is incomplete. Priors, shrinkage and posterior widths decide how hard to lean on a signal, and how much to size it.

Σ̂ = δ·F + (1 − δ)·S

Regularise before you optimise

With few observations per parameter, raw sample estimates overfit. I use Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage and L1/L2 penalties, and I control turnover explicitly.

SRnet = E[r − c] / σ(r)

Costs are part of the model

A backtest that ignores commission, slippage and turnover is measuring something else. I evaluate strategies net of costs, and only out of sample.

dXt = b(Xt, t) dt + σ dWt

Understand the process, then learn it

I work through the stochastic calculus and the Markov structure first (Schrödinger bridges, diffusions), so that the learned models behave sensibly outside the training range.

05 / About

Outside the models

I studied computer science and French at Shanghai Jiao Tong University before joining the engineering cycle at École Polytechnique in Paris. I've gone from nuclear-reactor digital twins to financial time series, and the question has stayed the same: what can you infer about a hidden state from noisy data, and how sure should you be?

Away from the desk I train seriously. I won the 2023 Shanghai Intercollegiate Bodybuilding Cup, captained the SPEIT fitness team, led the calisthenics association, and compete in CrossFit and Hyrox. I've also played rugby and American football. Training has taught me to run long experiments, keep a log, and accept slow, noisy feedback.
