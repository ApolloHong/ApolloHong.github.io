Schrödinger bridges for tail-risk scenarios on SPY

Supervised by Prof. Huyên Pham. A Schrödinger-bridge model of the full SPY return distribution under stochastic volatility and jump-diffusion, used to generate stress scenarios.

  • Lowest Wasserstein distance of the models benchmarked; matched jump frequency and 95th-percentile jump size

03 / Approach

Working habits

Financial data is noisy and non-stationary, and signals decay once they are traded. These are the habits I try to keep because of that.

\[p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D}) \;\propto\; p(\mathcal{D} \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)\]

Report the uncertainty with the estimate

A point forecast is not enough to size a position. Priors, shrinkage and posterior widths decide how much weight a signal gets.

\[\hat{\Sigma} \;=\; \delta\, F + (1-\delta)\, S\]

Regularise before optimising

With few observations per parameter, sample estimates overfit, so I use Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage, penalties and explicit turnover limits.

\[\mathrm{IR}_{\text{net}} \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}[\,r - c\,]}{\sigma(r - c)}\]

Evaluate net of costs

At SAIF, new features raised mean rank IC from 0.031 to 0.038, but after turnover limits, shrinkage and costs, net IR only went from 0.78 to 0.91.

\[dX_t \;=\; b(X_t, t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t)\,dW_t + dJ_t\]

Derive before fitting

For the SPY Schrödinger bridge, working out the drift and jump-intensity adjustments under the change of measure is what led me to the discretisation bias in simulated jump risk.
